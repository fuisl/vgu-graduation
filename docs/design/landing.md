# Landing teaser — GRAD ’26

The public root route is a quiet teaser for the November ceremony. It is the one place where the visual system permits a continuous 3D moment. Invitation and documentation routes remain separate.

## Approved visual direction

- Near-black surface: `#070A12`, with a restrained blue-violet radial field. The field itself is not static: three large, softly-blurred aurora blobs (reusing the same blue-violet palette as the sculpture's halo) drift and scale on slow, independent loops (39-58s) behind a 64px grid that itself pans on a 48s loop. All motion is small and slow by design — it should read as "the page is alive," not as an animated background competing with the sculpture. Disabled entirely under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`.
- ASCII characters use violet in shadow (`#856BD1`), blue in midtone (`#709EF5`), and icy cyan in highlight (`#C7F5FF`). These are the shader’s nominal RGB values. Color follows the sculpture’s lighting, not its screen position. This palette belongs to the landing sculpture only; it does not change the shared design tokens.
- Copy stays still, editorial, and small in scale. The primary line is exactly **“Coming soon in November.”** It has no year or signup action. Object names remain in the accessible description, with no visible caption.
- The framing reads a white `VGU` wordmark and `GRAD ’26` in the header only; there is no footer and no kicker line above the sculpture. The focal point is the sculpture, with generous dark space around it. The wordmark's acronym letters (V, G, U) anchor the display name; it reads out in full as "Vietnamese-German University" at rest, and compacts to "VGU" once the page scrolls, growing back out on hover so the full name can still be peeked while scrolled. Both directions use a brief per-character ascii-noise decode rather than a slide or fade.

## Sculpture behavior

The six deterministic point sets have the same count and cycle in this order: laptop; gradient-descent landscape with a descent path and three graph axes anchored to the surface's true bounding corner; a triangular lattice cell tower on a square base, with a dish mounted partway up one leg and a domed antenna ringed by signal arcs on top, standing in for electrical/electronics work; database → transform → database pipeline with directional chevrons; candlesticks with bodies, wicks, a subdued order-book base, and a bright diagonal trend arrow riding above the highs to make the uptrend an explicit annotation rather than an implicit read of the candle data; and, closing the cycle, a light bulb standing for innovation — one big, nearly-full glass globe with a zigzag filament, pinching through a short neck down to a small, clearly separate threaded screw base. Earlier revisions of this set used a Fibonacci-node sphere for AI/neural networks and, separately, a lightning bolt and a flat circuit board for electrical work; those were dropped for reading poorly while rotating or for not fitting the rest of the set once the bulb was added for innovation. The bulb itself also went through two revisions: an initial pass with thin rays off the top and a smaller, more cut-off globe read as noisy and lopsided through the ASCII sampling, so the rays were dropped and the globe was enlarged and rounded out instead; then the flat, uniformly-lit glass was given a fresnel-style shade instead — bright at the silhouette, dimmer toward the center-facing zone so it reads as a bit see-through, plus a directional shadow across it — and a sparse glow shell floats just outside the glass as an aura, both computed from the point's true position relative to the globe's center rather than the generic per-shape directional formula the rest of the set uses. Each form holds for five seconds and reforms over 1.5 seconds.

The same physically-derived shading was then extended to the rest of the set, each keyed to the part of its geometry with a real curved surface, using the point's position relative to that surface's own center rather than the flat per-shape directional formula: the cell tower's dome gets the same fresnel rim as the bulb's glass, and its signal arcs are shaded as a glow shell fading outward from the dome exactly like the bulb's aura; the database drums get a cylindrical fresnel rim, bright at each drum's silhouette edge and dim on the face turned toward the viewer; the laptop screen gets a backlight bloom, brightest at its center and fading toward the edges; and the candlestick trend arrow gets a spark of glow at its tip that fades back along the shaft. Parts without a natural curved surface — legs, braces, the base plate, the transform box, candle bodies — keep the plain directional formula.

The object wobbles rather than turning as a full turntable — rotation.y and rotation.x each follow a slow sine oscillation, so it never turns fully away from the camera — and layers two more motions on top: a per-point shimmer (a small sine-driven jitter, unique phase per point) that runs continuously, including while a shape is held rather than only during morphs, and a gentle whole-object breathing scale. Both are intentionally subtle so the held shape stays readable.

The scene is sampled into a fixed ASCII grid by a GPU shader. Light and shadow determine both glyph density and color. The implementation caps device pixel ratio at 1.25, uses smaller cells on phones, and pauses when the document is hidden. A static ASCII motif is in server-rendered HTML and remains visible for reduced motion, missing WebGL, render errors, or context loss.
