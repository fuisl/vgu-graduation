# Live surfaces: event display and captions

Two pages run during the ceremony: `/display` on the projector in the hall (#66) and `/live` on guests' phones (#70). Both reach the API directly (ADR-003: streams go browser-direct, `NEXT_PUBLIC_API_ORIGIN`), keep working through poor venue Wi-Fi, and never affect another page. The client logic is pure, unit-tested modules in `apps/web/lib/live/`.

## `/display` (event display)

- **Layout.** No site header or footer. A full-bleed blue screen in a grid: a top row (compact mark, event name, connection state), the stage, a share rail with a QR code to `/wishes`, and a deep-blue caption band along the bottom while captions flow. All type is Inter Tight sized in `vmin` with `clamp()` bounds, so it reads from the back of a hall at 1920x1080 and scales down to a phone (portrait stacks the QR row under the stage). No mono labels: nothing small enough to need one.
- **Stage.** One slide at a time: an approved wish as a large quote (five type steps by length, so a 1,000-character wish still fits) or a guest-camera photo (`display` derivative, contained, never cropped). New arrivals jump the queue, then everything cycles least-recently-shown first, alternating wishes and photos. A new arrival also cuts short a slide that is a repeat once it has been up 4 s (ADR-014); a slide's first showing always keeps its full time. A wish stays up 8 to 20 s by length, a photo 9 s. A slide a moderator hides leaves at once.
- **Empty and loading state.** "Welcome to", the event name in display size, the date, and "Scan the code to leave a wish." It is also what shows while connecting, so the screen is never blank.
- **Captions.** The display feed carries `segment` messages with every language. The band shows the newest line large and the one before it muted; drafts are muted until final. `?captions=` picks the language: `auto` (default) shows the other hall language (Vietnamese while English is spoken, English while Vietnamese is spoken), or `en`, `vi`, `de`, `off`. The band hides after 45 s without a new line and never shows when translation is absent. It also points phones at `/live`.
- **Connection state.** Top right, a small square dot and a word: Live (the one accent use on this screen), Connecting, Reconnecting, Offline (showing the last update), or Not signed in. The last content stays on screen while offline.
- **Motion.** Slides crossfade over `--motion-slow`; with reduced motion they cut (the leaving slide is never rendered).
- **Keyboard.** `F` toggles full screen; a "Full screen (F)" button appears when focused.

### Running unattended

The display must run for hours without leaking: at most two slides in the DOM, wishes and photos capped at 150 each (newest kept), caption segments capped at 4, the "recently shown" and "failed image" maps pruned to what is on the deck, one rotation timer, photo preloads that are aborted when superseded, one socket with at most two timers (`LiveSocket`) plus one 5-second catch-up poll that only fetches while the socket is down (ADR-014), and the Screen Wake Lock re-acquired when the tab becomes visible. A 10-minute soak against the fast mock (with the API killed and restarted midway) is the check; see "Verifying" below.

### Access

The display feed keeps the invitation credential (#65): photos are guest-only until flagged public, so the screen shows nothing to a browser without an invitation. The kiosk gets its own invitation and opens its link once in the kiosk browser; the `inv` cookie on the parent domain then rides on the socket handshake and the catch-up request. A browser without it sees the empty state and an operator notice ("This screen is not signed in"), and the page keeps retrying, so opening the link in another tab and reloading is enough. There is no separate display key: one more credential type would have to be minted, stored and rotated for a single screen, and the invitation already does that job. Captions on `/live` are public (#68) and need nothing.

## `/live` (captions on phones)

- **Layout.** Shared site header, a compact blue band (eyebrow, "Live captions", one lead line, connection state), then the caption log on white. No footer: the page is anchored to its live edge at the bottom. A white dock fixed to the bottom (an `aside` labelled "Caption settings", so nothing sits outside a landmark), clear of the home indicator (`env(safe-area-inset-*)`), holds the language toggle (DE, EN, VI; each named code first, "DE Deutsch", so the name starts with the visible text) and the text size toggle (three steps up to the h2 size), both `PillGroup as="toggle"` within thumb reach. Choices are remembered in `localStorage` (wrapped in try/catch); the first visit picks the browser's language if it is one of the three.
- **Captions.** One line per spoken sentence in the chosen language. A draft or a line still waiting for its translation shows lighter (the source text stands in until the translation arrives) and is replaced in place by the final. A line whose translation failed says so and shows the original. At most 300 lines are kept.
- **Following.** The page follows new lines while the reader is at the bottom; scrolling up stops it and shows a "Jump to live" button above the dock.
- **Screen readers.** The visible log is a `role="log"` wrapper with `aria-live="off"` around the `<ol>` (so the list stays a list). Final lines are announced through a separate polite live region, batched at most every 5 s and capped at 280 characters, so a fast speaker cannot flood the queue; the burst of recent lines sent on connect is not announced.
- **States.** Connecting, Live, "Waiting for the first words" (connected, nothing said yet), Reconnecting, and **Translation unavailable**: a notice explaining that the ceremony carries on and the page reconnects by itself, shown when the API reports `unavailable` (service suspended) or answers but has no translation. **Offline** (#78, like `/display`): when the browser reports it is offline, or the socket keeps failing and the catch-up request gets no answer at all, the status says Offline and a notice says the captions cannot be reached, to check Wi-Fi or mobile data, and that the page reconnects by itself (with lines on screen: that they may be out of date). Earlier lines stay readable.
- **Protocol.** `WS /live/translation` without `lang`, so every caption language arrives and switching language needs no refetch (a line is a few hundred bytes every few seconds). After a reconnect the page fetches `GET /live/translation/segments?since=<last until>` (up to three pages of 200). When the socket cannot open (the API answers 503 above `LIVE_TRANSLATION_MAX_CLIENTS`, or the network blocks WebSockets) it polls that endpoint every 5 s and returns to the socket as soon as one opens. Merging is idempotent by (sessionId, sequence) and never replaces a final with a draft.
- **Wake lock** while the page is open and visible.
- **Navigation.** Not in the menubar (it keeps to the event essentials, Camera and Wishes). Linked from the invitation's event details and from the display's caption band.

## `/gallery` (live, ADR-014)

The gallery's data comes from `useLiveGallery` (`apps/web/lib/live/use-live-gallery.ts`): first paint from the BFF's `/api/gallery`, then `WS /live/gallery` straight from the API with the same reconnect, catch-up (`GET /live/gallery/feed?since=`) and 5-second polling fallback as `/live`. New wishes and photos appear within about a second; a takedown disappears as fast. The guest's own photo shows from its local preview with `pending: "developing"` until the processed one arrives (`lib/memories/own-posts.ts`). The hook also returns `link` (`connecting`, `live`, `polling`, `offline`). The feed is used on `/gallery` (photos) and `/wishes` (wishes); how it looks is in `memories-archive.md` ("Digital Locket"). In short, **for owner review**: a developing photo is its preview in greyscale with a mono "Developing" tag, not openable, and turns into the processed photo in colour; new arrivals go straight in while the top of the feed is in view, and while the guest reads further down they are held behind a sticky "N new photos" / "N new wishes" button (zero-height holder, so neither the button nor the arrivals move the feed), released by the button (which scrolls to the feed heading and focuses it) or by scrolling back up; the guest's own posts are never held. `link` shows only as one muted line, "You're offline. New posts appear when you're back online.", when it is `offline`; `connecting`, `live` and `polling` show nothing.

## Verifying without the API

`apps/web/scripts/mock-live-api.mjs` is a dependency-free dev mock of the live endpoints, `GET /event` and generated photo derivatives (see `docs/development/local-development.md`). Scenarios: `--display=busy|empty|signin`, `--translation=live|unavailable|absent|full`, `--fast` for a 4x feed. Kill and restart it to watch both pages reconnect and catch up.
